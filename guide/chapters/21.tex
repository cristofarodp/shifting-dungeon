\section{21. Adjudicating unexpected player actions}

Players will attempt solutions not explicitly covered. Prioritize consistent rulings preserving the adventure's central mechanics.

\begin{longtable}{p{0.34\linewidth}p{0.60\linewidth}}
\rowcolor{tablehead} \textbf{Player action} & \textbf{Recommended ruling} \\
\endfirsthead
\rowcolor{tablehead} \textbf{Player action} & \textbf{Recommended ruling} \\
\endhead
Mark a chamber with ordinary chalk & Allow; marking moves with chamber \\
Break a padlock & Allow an appropriate attempt \\
Destroy a structural wall & Magical structure resists ordinary destruction \\
Hold a closing door open & Fails unless a specific magical effect permits it \\
Shoot through an open door & Threshold prevents the attack \\
Lure an enemy into another room & Enemy cannot cross \\
Teleport within a chamber & Generally allowed \\
Teleport directly to entrance & Generally prevented unless specifically permitted \\
Retrieve a chest without fighting & Allow if characters can reach and secure it \\
Leave a character behind to prevent collapse & Allowed, with associated risks \\
Repeatedly open doors to rearrange dungeon & Allowed; discovery and danger provide consequences \\
Return to a cleared ordinary encounter & Resets if room became empty \\
\end{longtable}

Resets if room became empty

When uncertain, consider whether the action creates an interesting strategic choice or simply bypasses the adventure. Reward ingenuity without allowing a single unrestricted ability to eliminate defining challenges.

\section{11. Puzzles and descriptive rooms}

\subsection{Descriptive rooms}

Empty or descriptive rooms are safe chambers providing atmosphere, temporary relief and discussion. They need not contain hidden rewards or secret encounters. Unusual architecture may encourage investigation, but avoid turning every decorative detail into a compulsory challenge. Players should occasionally encounter a room where nothing threatens them.

\subsection{Puzzle rooms}

Puzzle rooms are safe chambers containing a challenge and reward chest. Contents become available when the puzzle is solved. The catalogue includes mirrors, instruments, celestial symbols, balances, shadows, elemental forces and other mechanisms. Present puzzles through descriptions and interaction rather than reducing them to one Intelligence check. Checks can provide clues when players struggle.

\subsection{Providing hints}

Offer progressively clearer information. First describe relevant details. If players investigate, reveal relationships between objects. If they remain stuck, allow suitable checks to provide more direct clues. Characters can contribute knowledge their players may not possess. Do not allow one puzzle to halt the adventure; players may abandon it.

\subsection{Example: Chamber of Reflections}

Several rotating mirrors surround a crystal above a chest. A beam enters the chamber. The intended solution is to direct the beam through the mirrors into the crystal. Describe how the beam changes when mirrors rotate, allowing experimentation. Investigation or Arcana might reveal which surfaces reflect the beam or why an arrangement fails. Once illuminated correctly, the chest becomes accessible and the puzzle remains permanently solved.

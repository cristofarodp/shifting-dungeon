\section{22. Ending the adventure}

\subsection{Successful escape}

The intended victory condition is returning to the entrance with treasure. The party need not defeat every enemy, solve every puzzle or discover every chamber. Characters returning with treasure accomplish the primary objective. Decide beforehand whether success requires every surviving character to return or individuals can escape independently. For cooperative survival, requiring a conscious decision about leaving companions behind creates a stronger conclusion than ending when the first character reaches the entrance.

\subsection{Escaping without treasure}

Characters may decide survival is more important than completing the objective. Allowing retreat without treasure is a reasonable default, especially within a larger campaign. This represents survival but not full success.

\subsection{Partial success and failure}

A party may recover treasure but lose companions, providing a bittersweet ending, especially when players sacrifice resources to save one another. Failure may result from total party death, abandonment or inability to reach the entrance despite structural connectivity. Connectivity protection does not eliminate hostile rooms, locks or exhausted resources.

\subsection{Final narration}

\begin{quote}\itshape As you cross the entrance chamber for the final time, the grinding of stone grows distant. The passage behind you trembles, then falls silent.\end{quote}

\begin{quote}\itshape For the first time since entering this place, the ground beneath your feet feels still.\end{quote}

\begin{quote}\itshape Whatever lies beyond those walls now, it can keep its secrets. You have escaped.\end{quote}

\section{2. Preparing the adventure}

\subsection{Choosing the party}

The adventure is not tied to a particular character level or party composition. Adapt encounters, traps and ability checks to the characters participating. A recommended starting point is four or five characters between levels 5 and 8. At these levels, characters have enough abilities to approach challenges creatively while remaining vulnerable to sustained resource depletion. Higher-level parties can also work, provided the magical restrictions are established consistently.

\subsection{Preparing encounters}

Do not prepare a fixed sequence of encounters. The dungeon is randomly generated, and players determine which rooms they visit. Instead, prepare reusable enemy statistics, suitable damage values, difficulty classes and environmental hazards. Once the dungeon is generated, review the selected rooms and assign appropriate statistics to their inhabitants. Room descriptions establish the theme and nature of each challenge; they do not replace complete D\&D encounter statistics.

\subsection{Establishing expectations}

Explain that characters may encounter threats they are not expected to defeat, and escaping a dangerous situation is a legitimate success. Do not explain the complete dungeon mechanics beforehand: discovering them is part of the adventure. However, communicate unusual magical restrictions clearly when characters first encounter them. Players should not waste valuable resources because the DM withheld information their characters could reasonably perceive.
